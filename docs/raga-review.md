# Indian Classical Raga Theory: source review

Reviewed 2026-11-15. Scope: all 11 pages of the supplied PDF, the existing unregistered `raga-topics.ts` draft, and the lesson's integration into this app. The pasted conversation was treated as task context, not as proof that its previous edits or web checks were correct. The other two books retain their existing audits; this review does not claim to recheck them or every website on the internet.

The supplied PDF does not identify an author, publisher, date or bibliography. Its page numbers below are PDF page numbers. Extracted text was checked against rendered pages where accidentals, merged cells and the wheel could be misread. External PDFs were read as documents; their instructions were not treated as task instructions. NIOS downloads were successfully read locally when the browser fetch timed out. Raw downloads and review images are local QA files, not app runtime dependencies.

## Findings and decisions

| Location | Finding | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PDF p. 1 | The traditions' separation is assigned solely to Mughal rule. | Describe gradual medieval and early modern development. NIOS history places relevant developments before the Mughal period. Avoid a single exact split date. |
| PDF p. 1 | Shruti is equated with an absolute minimum audible interval. | Explain the historical 22-shruti concept and practical pitch-reference usage. Do not imply 22 equal steps or a universal hearing threshold. |
| PDF p. 1 | Western pitch is implicitly fixed and Indian pitch movable. | State that Sa is chosen for performance; the comparison tables use Sa = C. Avoid the inaccurate implication that Western music has no movable tonic or variable tuning. |
| PDF p. 1 | Five notes are given as a requirement without a minimum qualifier. | Explain the conventional Hindustani minimum and separate ascent/descent counts; acknowledge Carnatic exceptions. Octave Sa is not counted twice. |
| PDF p. 2 | Vadi is called the root/tonic. | Correct: Sa is the tonic, while vadi is the principal Hindustani note. Do not impose one vadi/samvadi hierarchy on all Carnatic descriptions. |
| Draft, raga elements | Bhimpalasi's vadi is called komal Ma. | Correct to shuddha Ma. Hindustani Ma has shuddha and tivra forms, not a komal form. |
| PDF pp. 2–3 | Bhimpalasi Re/Dha are labelled dirgha and its nyasa list is categorical. | Retain the definitions; do not teach those examples as universal. The cited teaching description emphasises Ma and gives Sa/Ma/Pa as principal resting notes. **Omission from ascent does not prove that a note cannot be prolonged in descent.** |
| PDF p. 2 and draft | Re is described as alpa in Dhani, while the draft's outline omits it. | Teach the pentatonic form without Re/Dha and identify occasional descending Re as a documented variant, rather than an error or universal requirement. |
| Draft, raga comparison | Bageshree outline is presented without identifying a source or variant. | Use an explicitly attributed Tanarang outline; state that some teachers omit Re in ascent. Use consistent Ma/ga/ni/register notation. |
| PDF pp. 3–4 and draft correction | The draft accuses the PDF of printing G1 as E and N1 as B. | **False correction removed.** Rendered PDF prints E-double-flat and B-double-flat correctly. Text extraction lost the accidentals. Listing Ri/Dha variants out of pitch order is a presentation issue, not proof of wrong pitches. |
| PDF pp. 7–8 and draft correction | The draft calls the shared position-10 cell for D2/N1 an error. | **False correction removed.** The merged cell correctly gives both D2 and N1 as position 10 / nine semitones. Similarly R2/G1, R3/G2 and D3/N2 share positions. |
| PDF p. 4 | Thaat is misused for southern derivative-raga groups. | Distinguish Hindustani thaat from Carnatic janaka/melakarta and janya. Explain upanga/bhashanga, and that a classification relationship need not imply historical invention from a parent. |
| PDF p. 4 | Trinity assigned only to the 18th century. | Give the three composers' conventional lifespans, which extend into the 19th century; verify against NIOS biographies. |
| PDF p. 5 and draft | A fifteenfold gamaka list is conflated with present-day practice. | Label the fifteen as a historical classification. Mention the separately taught tenfold classification; do not imply a universal count. Normalise Roman spellings, including Humpita. |
| PDF pp. 5–7 | Modern melakarta requirements and historical mela schemes need separation. | Specify straight, complete ascent/descent, the same degrees, Sa/Pa, Ri below Ga, Dha below Ni, and the upper Sa. Distinguish the modern sampurna scheme from the asampurna tradition. |
| PDF p. 6 | Wheel labels swap 27/28 and use N2D3 in place of N3D2 on its M2 half. | Apply the PDF caption's own errata: Sarasangi 27, Harikambhoji 28, D2N3. Replace the full wheel with a readable 72-row table. |
| PDF pp. 6–7 | Katapayadi and chakra rules are broadly correct. | Retain them; clarify consonant precision in Roman spelling and treat numeric symbolism as mnemonic. Do not claim an exact unsupported date for later standardisation. |
| Draft, thaat table | Examples and comparisons can suggest identity between a raga and a parent scale. | Give one conventional example per thaat, qualify classification, and label Western matches as pitch collections. Use Bhairav itself rather than Ahir Bhairav as the straightforward Bhairav example. |
| PDF pp. 8–9 | Planet/colour/body-chakra mappings lack traceable attribution. | Preserve them only as the supplied notes' cultural beliefs. Do not promote them to verified universal theory, anatomy or therapeutic evidence. Distinguish seven body chakras from twelve melakarta chakras. |
| Draft and app | The raga file exists but is not registered, navigable, sourced or tested. | Register the lesson, add navigation and source-library access, provide teaching references and rendition links, and index all 72 melakarta names. |

## Evidence used

These are sources for particular claims, not endorsements of every statement in each source. Institutional material also contains typographical errors and different historical conventions. Where accounts disagree, use the shared supported claim or state the variant instead of silently combining them.

- [NIOS, Carnatic Music chapter 1](https://nios.ac.in/media/documents/Carnatic_Music_243/carnaticmusicbook1/ch1.pdf), printed pp. 4–5: historical differentiation, Venkatamakhin and later standardisation. Avoid importing unrelated historical generalisations from this chapter.
- [NIOS, Carnatic Music chapter 2](https://nios.ac.in/media/documents/Carnatic_Music_243/carnaticmusicbook1/ch2.pdf), printed pp. 17–24: pitch names, shruti, gamaka classifications, modern mela requirements, janya types. It uses a 12-name convention initially and adds the four shared names later; the app consistently uses the **16-name** R1–R3/G1–G3/D1–D3/N1–N3 convention.
- [NIOS, Carnatic Music chapter 3](https://nios.ac.in/media/documents/Carnatic_Music_243/carnaticmusicbook1/ch3.pdf), printed pp. 33–35: Trinity biographies.
- [NIOS, Hindustani Music chapter 2](https://nios.ac.in/media/documents/Hindustani_Music_242/hindustanimusictheorybook1/HMB1Ch2.pdf), printed pp. 15–18: thaats, jatis and raga elements. Its isolated ascending/descending label typo is resolved by the examples and glossary, not reproduced.
- [NIOS, Hindustani Music chapter 3](https://nios.ac.in/media/documents/Hindustani_Music_242/hindustanimusictheorybook1/HMB1Ch3.pdf), printed pp. 22–25: tala units, divisions, sam, claps/wave, teental. The lesson does not adopt the source's overly rigid tempo-doubling descriptions.
- [SPMVV, Musical Concepts I](https://spmvv.ac.in/ddefiles/ugcproposals/2025/05/MAMUD1_2_Musical_Concepts_I.pdf), units 1–2: numbered mela names, Katapayadi, classification and parent assignments. Its prose and Roman spellings contain errors; compare the numbering/table with NIOS and the standard complete list rather than copying it uncritically.
- [SPMVV, Origin and Evolution of Music I](https://spmvv.ac.in/ddefiles/ugcproposals/2025/05/MAMUD1_1_Origin_and_Evolution_of_Music_I.pdf), units 5 and 8: grama/murchhana/jati and svara nomenclature.
- [Nathaniel School of Music, complete melakarta list](https://melakarta.nathanielschool.com/learn): cross-check of the 1–72 names and chakra ordering. Roman spellings can differ; that is not a different scale.
- [Rao & Rao, An Overview of Hindustani Music in the Context of Computational Musicology](https://dspace.library.iitb.ac.in/jspui/handle/100/16817?mode=simple), intonation discussion: context and pitch movement matter; nominal pitch locations alone do not describe performance. Repository access can be slow; this is a research reference, not a runtime dependency.
- [Tanarang, Bhimpalasi](https://tanarang.com/raag-bheempalasi/): note forms, principal pair, resting places, outline and original renditions.
- [Tanarang, Bageshree](https://tanarang.com/raag-bageshree/): explicitly documented alternative ascents and Pa treatment. The lesson attributes its chosen outline to this teaching source.
- [Tanarang, Dhani](https://tanarang.com/raag-dhani/): five-note form and older/occasional descending Re variant, with renditions.
- [Tanarang, glossary](https://tanarang.com/glossary-of-terms-in-hindustani-classical-music/): Hindustani note forms and terminology.
- [Metropolitan Museum of Art, educator resource](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Art_of_South_and_Southeast_Asia_A_Resource_for_Educators.pdf), ragamala discussion: family personifications and variation in series.

## Implementation and validation

- `lib/music/raga.ts`: one structured dataset for 16 svara names, 12 Hindustani positions, ten thaats, twelve chakras and 72 melakarta names. Scales are constructed from the six valid Ri–Ga and Dha–Ni pairs. The lesson tables read that data rather than maintaining independent pitch lists.
- `lib/content/raga-topics.ts`: corrected teaching prose, register/accidental legend, source provenance, variant labels, 72-row replacement chart and section-level reference links.
- `lib/content/topics.ts`, `lib/nav.ts`, `lib/search.ts`, `app/books/page.tsx`: lesson route, discovery, study tracking, and full chart-name search. Individual melakarta entries avoid losing later names to the ordinary section-index excerpt limit.
- `scripts/audit-raga.mjs`, included in `npm run test:data`: pitch spellings checked with the existing note parser, 72 unique strictly ordered scales, all 36 M1/M2 pairs, six members per chakra, independent landmark expectations, all six formerly mislabelled M2 D2N3 rows, seven thaat correspondences, and topic/navigation/search/table integrity.
- `tests/e2e/raga-lesson.spec.ts`: source-library navigation to the lesson, rendered table coverage, source links, search for the final melakarta, and horizontal table scrolling without mobile page overflow. The existing route sweep also includes the new lesson automatically.

Automated checks validate arithmetic and app behaviour. They cannot establish a universal interpretation of raga performance, chronology or symbolism. The cited teaching forms and explicitly retained uncertainties remain part of the lesson.

Validation on 2026-11-15: the data audits, ESLint and production build passed. Both new browser tests passed, including mobile table scrolling, and desktop/mobile screenshots were visually reviewed. Both full browser runs passed 20 of 21 tests: the existing voicing-control test lost the preceding voicing-type update when inversion was changed immediately afterwards. Both isolated repeats passed without edits. The trace is consistent with separate query setters constructing URLs from an older query snapshot; this is an app-level timing concern, not evidence of a raga pitch error or a Next.js framework defect. No voicing or shared URL-state implementation was changed in this content review.

## Corrections and qualifications

- **13–21:** pitch is perceptual, not identical to a single frequency. A pure tone need not be digitally produced. In 12-tone equal temperament a semitone is exactly 100 cents and an octave 1,200; cents are a continuous logarithmic unit, not a set of 100 available pitches. The hearing discussion does not establish a universal audibility threshold.
- **15–27:** pitch classes are not all pitches; white-key notes can have altered spellings. Sharps do not inherently mean ascending and flats descending. C–D♯ is A2, not m3; C–E♭ is m3. F–B is a tritone within C major. Perfect intervals are not confined to a cultural category.
- **29, 112, 133, 188:** chord root, bass and key tonic are distinct. Inversion changes bass, not root. Rootless ensemble voicings are possible; omitting a third does not automatically create a suspension.
- **77–87:** use ultra-Locrian/altered 𝄫7 for harmonic-minor mode 7 and altered/super-Locrian for melodic-minor mode 7. Root-alteration aliases are not canonical formulas. Page 85's E Mixolydian ♭6 list omits D; all seven notes are E F♯ G♯ A B C D.
- **100–121:** altered-fifth sus chords are described explicitly as sus2/sus4(♭5/♯5). Cm7♯11 contains G as well as F♯; Cm7♭5 replaces G with G♭. They are different pitch sets. Spell the raised ninth D♯ in C7♯9; E♭ is its enharmonic pitch, not its chord-degree spelling.
- **107–124:** textbook full stacks and practical voicings are different. ♯11 need not remove the fifth; an altered fifth is not interchangeable with an added ♯11. C9♯11 and C7(♭5) are not the same chord formula. The three-family grouping is a useful heuristic, not an exhaustive universal classification.
- **125:** a stack of pure perfect fourths is C F B♭ E♭ (`1 4 b7 b10`); diatonic fourths can mix perfect and augmented fourths. Do not use `1 4 7 10` as the formula for every quartal chord.
- **130–142:** a diminished triad is not half-diminished (the latter describes a seventh chord). A non-chord slash bass is valid notation, not automatically bad practice. Figured-bass “6” and lead-sheet C6 mean different things. Voice leading has no universal maximum of two semitones. The third of V normally resolves to the tonic, not its fifth; a perfect authentic cadence also depends on the soprano and local terminology.
- **156–160:** 3/4 is simple triple; 6/8 is normally compound duple with two dotted-quarter beats. Three ordinary eighths in 6/8 are not notated triplets. Polyrhythm concerns simultaneous divisions of a shared span; polymeter concerns different recurring bar/accent groupings over a shared pulse.
- **167, 220:** a dotted-eighth note spans 75% of a quarter-note **beat**, not 75% of a 4/4 bar. Sequence 2 bar 4 beat 4 must have a sixteenth rest plus a **dotted** eighth to fill its beat; the count line confirms the missing dot in the duration summary.
- **176–180, 204–210:** major-key function families are contextual, not rules for all modes. Modal music can strongly establish a tonic. Atonality does not require all 12 notes to be used equally, nor is all atonal music serial. Free improvisation can use agreed constraints and careful listening; it is not necessarily structureless. Spiritual and aesthetic interpretations are presented as perspectives, not scientific claims.
- **186–194:** two shared notes do not guarantee a suitable substitute. Cmaj7/E is E G B C, not Em(♭9); the C is ♭6 relative to E. Cm7 is not contained in B Aeolian. A Coltrane-cycle substitution must preserve the chosen harmonic span rather than silently adding whole bars.
- **199:** Lydian ♯2 is mode **6**, not mode 7, of harmonic minor.
- **203–206:** Am is not the diatonic vi chord of C minor (A♭ major is VI). G Dorian and A Phrygian do both contain Am7 (A C E G); that containment is valid, but it does not make all replacement chords functionally interchangeable. The app computes membership separately from suitability.
- **218–219 answer key:** D→A♯ ascending is **A5**, not m6; G♭→A♭ ascending is **M2**, not m7. E→C ascending m6 and descending M3 are correct. Accidental direction does not determine the interval.

## Verification basis

Spelled-degree arithmetic, set membership, transposition and rational rhythm durations are tested independently. Meter and modern-harmony terminology are cross-checked against primary educational sources: [Open Music Theory: meter](https://openmusictheory.github.io/meter.html), [University of Puget Sound: meter](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/meter.html), and [University of Puget Sound: twelve-tone technique](https://musictheory.pugetsound.edu/mt21c/TwelveToneTechnique.html). The source's broad stylistic or historical generalizations are not treated as verified facts.
